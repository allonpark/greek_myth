# Mythos Atlas — 43-Episode Continuity Map

This file is the continuity spine for the revised edition. It preserves the published episode numbers 1–35 and fixes the remaining arc through Episode 43. It is an editorial map, not a primary source.

## Global narrative threads

- **Succession and inherited violence:** Uranus → Cronus → Zeus; royal houses; Oedipus; Atreids; Priam; later Roman foundation memory.
- **Mortality versus fame:** Heracles' apotheosis, Bellerophon, Achilles, Hector, Odysseus, Aeneas.
- **Hospitality, oath, gift, and obligation:** Pandora's gift, xenia, Tyndareus' oath, battlefield truces, Diomedes–Glaucus, Odyssey hospitality.
- **Women displaced by heroic decisions:** Pandora, Medea, Ariadne, Helen, Iphigenia, Briseis, Andromache, Penelope, Dido.
- **Burial and the rights of the dead:** Antigone → Patroclus → Hector/Priam → sack and aftermath.
- **Human/divine agency:** Prometheus, Typhon, Paris/Helen, Pandarus, Diomedes, Achilles.
- **Return (nostos) and failed return:** Argonaut return, Theseus' black sail, Protesilaus, Agamemnon, Odysseus, Aeneas.
- **Myth and material evidence:** Minotaur/Knossos, Troy/Wilusa, Mycenaean archives, Late Bronze Age collapse, Roman foundation traditions.

## Episodes 1–35: preserve published subjects, revise continuity

1. Chaos — seed the problem of order imposed on an uncertain beginning; payoff in Ep43's “myth → history” boundary.
2. Gaia/Uranus/Cronus — succession violence; echo later dynastic conflicts and inherited curses.
3. Cronus and Zeus — fear of replacement; later royal houses repeat the same logic without literal divine succession.
4. Titanomachy — divine war as a distant mirror for mortal coalition warfare, not a direct historical template.
5. Typhon — establish cross-cultural storm-god comparison method and its limits; reuse this method in Ep43.
6. Prometheus — human/divine boundary; payoff in Diomedes (33) and Achilles (38).
7. Pandora — gifts whose consequences exceed intention; echo golden apple (25) and war prizes (30/36).
8. Flood traditions — survival and refoundation; payoff in Aeneas/Rome (42) and historical-memory discussion (43).
9. Perseus/Medusa — monster, gaze, heroic violence, and changing reception layers; model for later source-layer reading.
10. Heracles — strength does not erase mortality or moral fracture; contrast Achilles' coming choice (36–38).
11. Bellerophon — preserve xenia and Lycia thread; explicit payoff at Diomedes–Glaucus (34).
12. Oedipus — inherited curse, knowledge arriving too late; resonance with Atreids and Trojan foreknowledge.
13. Seven Against Thebes — Diomedes' family background; payoff at Ep33 and the generational logic of war.
14. Antigone — burial obligation; major payoff at Hector/Priam in Ep39.
15. Argonaut coalition — prototype of pan-heroic gathering; distant resonance with Achaean coalition at Troy.
16. Argonaut return — return as a second ordeal; payoff in Nostoi/Odyssey at Ep41.
17. Pelias' death — homecoming can carry violence into the household; echo Agamemnon's nostos in Ep41.
18. Medea variants — teach readers not to harmonize versions; payoff in Helen traditions (26), Troy sack variants (40), Roman reception (42).
19. Theseus recognition — identity and recognition; payoff in Odysseus' return (41).
20. Minotaur/Knossos — archaeology can contextualize without proving a named myth; payoff in Ep43.
21. Black sail/Aegeus — misread signs and delayed knowledge; echo battlefield signals and homecoming recognition.
22. Hippolytus — oaths, curses, speech acts, and irreversible consequences; echo war oaths and divine-human causation.
23. Underworld descent — seed katabasis as a knowledge-space; payoff in Odyssey (41) and Aeneas (42).
24. Atalanta — marriage, agency, and imposed social order; bridge to Thetis' wedding (25).
25. Wedding of Thetis / golden apple — the chain that opens the Trojan arc; eventual payoff in Troy's destruction (40).
26. Helen — agency remains unresolved; return this ambiguity when survivors are redistributed after the sack (40).
27. Iphigenia — war begins by damaging the house that commands it; payoff in Agamemnon's return (41).
28. Protesilaus — first death and impossible return; resonance with the wider Nostoi (41).
29. Troilus — sanctuary, prophecy, Achilles' violence; shadow toward Achilles' own death (40).
30. Achilles' wrath — honor cannot be priced cleanly; immediate payoff in Embassy (36), catastrophic payoff in Patroclus (37).
31. Thersites — hierarchy controls who may speak truth; retain as counterpoint to elite negotiation in Ep36 and return narratives.
32. Paris/Menelaus truce — human agreements can fail under layered agency; echo final destruction of Troy.
33. Diomedes — divine permission temporarily opens the mortal/divine boundary seeded in Ep6.
34. Diomedes/Glaucus — xenia over battlefield identity; major payoff in Odyssey hospitality tests (41).
35. Hector/Andromache — family cost and foreknowledge; payoff in Hector's death/burial (38–39) and sack aftermath (40).

## Episodes 36–43: fixed remaining arc

   - Agamemnon's gifts, Phoenix, Ajax, Odysseus.
   - Achilles' two fates: short glorious life vs long obscure return.
   - Recover Pandora/gift, xenia, Thersites/status, Briseis/honor.
   - Do not resolve whether Achilles is “right.”

37. **When Another Man Wears Achilles' Armor — Patroclus (Iliad 11–16)**
   - Pressure at the ships, Patroclus' intervention, armor, Sarpedon, Apollo/Euphorbus/Hector, death.

   - End with Achilles receiving news, not with a moral.

38. **The Return of Achilles — Shield, River, Hector (Iliad 18–22)**

## Foreshadowing rule

Foreshadow only with information already available to the narrator/source tradition or with clearly marked prolepsis. Do not invent omens, hidden motives, or private thoughts merely to prepare later episodes. A good seed should be unobtrusive on first reading and gain weight only after the payoff episode.

## Payoff rule

When a later episode recovers an earlier thread, do not re-explain the entire earlier episode. Use one concrete object, phrase, obligation, wound, burial, gift, or relationship that changes meaning in the later context.

## Ending rule

Avoid a repeated “therefore the lesson is…” cadence. End with an unresolved pressure that genuinely belongs to the next source layer: a body not buried, a ship that has not returned, a promise whose price is not yet known, or an archaeological identification that remains probabilistic.
