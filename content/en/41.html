EPISODE 41 · NOSTOI & ODYSSEY

The War Is Over, but Is Home Still There? — The Returns and Odysseus' Homecoming

Nostoi · Agamemnon and Clytemnestra · Menelaus and Helen · Odysseus · xenia · underworld · Penelope's bed

When Troy burns, the victors do not all sail in one direction. The ancient summary of the lost Nostoi begins the returns with disagreement over how to appease Athena and when to depart; Agamemnon and Menelaus split, and the paths of the heroes scatter. [B] As the Argonauts learned in Episode 16, victory and return are not the same event.

Agamemnon receives the darkest homecoming. The Odyssey repeatedly sets his fate beside Odysseus' own return: Agamemnon reaches home and is killed in the plot of Aegisthus and Clytemnestra. [A] The wound opened in the house of Atreus before the war, in the Iphigenia traditions of Episode 27, erupts differently in later dramatic traditions. The Odyssey and later tragedians do not construct Clytemnestra's motive and responsibility in exactly the same way. [A/C]

Menelaus does not simply sail straight to Sparta either. In Odyssey 4 he tells of wandering as far as Egypt, and he is now home with Helen. [A] The question left open in Episode 26 remains unstable: Helen can be remembered as the figure around whom war was fought and still appear in the Odyssey as a queen who interprets memories and acts within the household. [A] Homecoming does not erase the past; it creates a new narrative layer in which people live after it.

Odysseus' path is more prolonged still. Cyclops, Aeolus, Laestrygonians, Circe, Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, Calypso: these are not only a catalogue of marvels but repeated structures of delay, diversion, loss, and failed departure. [A] The misread black sail of Episode 21 made a sign deadly. In the Odyssey, name, disguise, sign, and recognition become part of the architecture of return itself.

Xenia, which saved Diomedes and Glaucus in Episode 34, now becomes a vast test. Polyphemus eats guests; the Phaeacians receive a stranger and carry him home; the suitors occupy Odysseus' house and consume the absent master's property. [A] How a host treats a guest—and how a guest behaves in another man's house—becomes a measure not only of survival but of social order.

The underworld journey also recovers an older thread. Unlike Theseus' failed katabasis in Episode 23, Odysseus descends for knowledge. He consults Tiresias about the conditions of return and meets his mother and the war dead. [A] Agamemnon tells the catastrophe of his homecoming; Achilles famously values living life above rule among the dead. [A] The two fates of Episode 36 sound different after death.

Even when Odysseus reaches Ithaca, he does not immediately enter under his own name. He observes in disguise, and Eumaeus, Telemachus, and Eurycleia recognize or fail to recognize him in different ways. [A] Recognition saved Theseus in Episode 19 and armor created a false Achilles in Episode 37. Now Odysseus must hide who he is in order to recover the place where he belongs.

After the suitors are killed, one recognition still remains. Penelope does not abandon all doubt merely because someone says the man is Odysseus. She orders their bed moved as a test. Odysseus reacts because the bed cannot be moved without cutting the living olive tree around which he built it and the room itself. [A] Shared knowledge of construction proves identity more strongly than name or face.

Homecoming is therefore more than location. Odysseus' ship reached Ithaca earlier; the return becomes complete only when two people recognize a shared structure of memory. [D] The Argonaut return, Pelias' violent aftermath, Protesilaus' impossible return, and the black sail all made “going home” unstable in different ways. Odysseus succeeds, but many others do not.

For Trojan survivors, nostos is impossible in a more literal sense: the home itself has been destroyed. Aeneas therefore moves in another direction. He does not return to an old city; later tradition makes him an ancestor of a new one. But Dido, the Roman underworld, Latium, and the future of Rome are not all Homeric. The next episode depends on keeping those layers apart.

Reference & Evidence Notes

[B] Proclus, summary of the Nostoi. Ancient summary of the lost return epic and principal evidence for the scattered departures after Troy.

[A] Homer, Odyssey. Direct source for Odysseus' return, hospitality tests, underworld encounters, Agamemnon and Achilles among the dead, and the recognition scenes in Ithaca.

[A] Odyssey 23.181–204. Penelope's bed test and the secret of the rooted olive-tree bed.
